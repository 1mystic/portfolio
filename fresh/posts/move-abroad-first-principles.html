Should You Move Abroad? A First-Principles Take

Aug 30, 2026 • 7 min read • By Atharv Khare

Whenever a hot topic pops up, everyone has an opinion ready to blurt out before considering other perspectives.

Opinions are naturally biased because they stem directly from personal experiences. When it comes to moving abroad, Indian discourse usually splits into two extreme social narratives: either “life outside India is significantly better,” or “India has immense potential, so you must stay and contribute to the nation.”

Most people slowly drift toward one of these extremes. My habit is to reset and think about it from the ground up, stripping away whatever has been projected onto me. The flimsy idea of “duty to the nation” sounds noble, but it does not drive my choices. I perceive the entire planet as my boundary of life. I hold no misplaced pride attached to a specific geography, religion, or culture, which leaves me open to whatever this world has to offer.

The Two Narratives We Usually Fall Into

So how should someone decide whether to move abroad? Simple, you can start with a look at your available information, your current conditions, and your actual options. Check each option freely to see which one delivers your desired outcome with a clear purpose. Right now, many in India hold a romanticized notion that life abroad is universally flashy, clean, and comfortable. That is a tourist perspective. Experiencing a place as a visitor is fundamentally different from building a daily life there.

On the other hand, the patriotic narrative often ignores a basic truth: comfort, opportunity, and civil infrastructure are not evenly distributed across every city or every nation. When you decide based on slogans instead of live conditions, you end up optimizing for ideology instead of reality.

Starting from First Principles

When I evaluate my current situation from first principles, I look at what I actually want out of daily life: good food, family, a comfortable home, low pollution, and accessible green space.

My current city delivers all of this effortlessly. I get clean air for most of the year, distinct seasons, low pollution, and total daily convenience with quick-commerce deliveries within six minutes. Within a short radius, I have green society parks, public parks, malls, hundreds of cafes, fine-dining restaurants, five-star hotels, sports complexes, arcades, top-tier healthcare, and elite educational institutes spanning AIIMS, IISER, NIT, NLU, NID, NIFT, IIFM, SPA, and IIIT. The city even features a national park, massive lakes, and an expanding metro system.

Crucially, purchasing power parity here is extremely high. What costs 1 Crore in a congested tech hub like Bangalore can be accessed at the same or better luxury level here for just 20 Lakhs. Moving out simply to hit a baseline quality of life makes no sense when my environment already fulfills all my core needs and personal whims.

India Isn’t One Monolithic Choice

The mistake many make when weighing options inside India is looking exclusively at overcrowded, expensive metros like Delhi, Bangalore, or Mumbai. When people complain about urban friction or pollution, they forget that India has a wide spectrum of cities tailored to specific priorities:

  • Clean Air & Low Pollution: Places like Aizawl, Gangtok, Madikeri, and Shillong offer pristine air for those prioritizing respiratory health over big-city hustle.
  • Civic Cleanliness & Sanitation: Cities like Indore, Bhopal, Chandigarh, and Mysuru lead national standards in waste management and civic upkeep.
  • Nature & Green Living: Cities like Bhopal and Dehradun offer an exceptional mix of lush forest covers, water bodies, and quick access to nature while keeping full urban infrastructure intact.
  • Balanced All-Rounders: Options like Pune, Mysuru, Indore, Bhopal, and Chandigarh balance career access, top-tier healthcare, affordability, and manageable traffic without the burnout of Tier-1 metros.

Given that India is already the 3rd largest economy by PPP, moving abroad shouldn't be about seeking standard comfort or chasing a surface-level image. In host countries, you have to adjust to strict foreign rules, unfamiliar social structures, and cultural trade-offs, whereas living comfortably in India offers significant personal freedom.

The Real Question: What Purpose Is Missing?

The primary deciding factor for moving abroad should be a specific, non-negotiable purpose that India cannot currently fulfill. For instance, if your specialized field or domain requires an industry maturity or research infrastructure that India will take another 10 to 15 years to build, moving to where that ecosystem exists right now makes complete sense.

Granted, this remains my personal perspective, shaped by visiting countries abroad and observing relatives who have lived overseas for over 15 years across multiple nations.

The point is simple:
Don’t move abroad because social media says it is “better.”
Don’t stay in India because nationalism says it is “your duty.”
Move or stay only when the choice clearly serves your actual purpose.

Ultimately, the goal isn't to convince anyone to leave or stay. It is a matter of perspective: before reaching a decision, walk the path intellectually and test your assumptions without relying on noisy social narratives. Deciding where to live is entirely your choice. At the end of the day, there is only one Earth, and arbitrary boundaries shouldn't dictate your purpose.
